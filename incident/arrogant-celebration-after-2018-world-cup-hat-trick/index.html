Arrogant Celebration After 2018 World Cup Hat-Trick

Location Russia (Portugal vs Spain)
Category National Team
Severity

The hat-trick against Spain: In the 2018 World Cup group stage, Portugal drew Spain 3-3, and Ronaldo scored all three — a penalty, a left-footed thunderbolt, an 88th-minute free-kick equaliser. The first hat-trick ever scored against Spain at a World Cup, and every one of Portugal's goals on the night.

Cocky celebration: More viral than the goals was the post-goal chin-stroking celebration, widely read as an 'I'm the best in history' declaration. The pose didn't so much brush the line between confidence and arrogance as set up camp on the far side of it.

Self-anointed 'GOAT': In post-match interviews and later documentaries Ronaldo styled himself the 'best player in history' on repeat, with the air of a man without a peer. The self-coronation rests on ability; it also gives off a discomforting narcissism.

Hero versus team exit: For all the deified hat-trick, Portugal still went out in the round of 16 of that World Cup. One man's brilliance could not mask the team's mediocrity, and the 'best in history' performance could not deliver a World Cup trophy.

Messi mirror image: The media reached for the inevitable comparison — Messi, equally outstanding at that year's World Cup and far more restrained about it. Some fans read Ronaldo's bravado as authenticity; others as the pathology of excessive self-centredness.

The danger of 'I am history': When an athlete wears 'best in history' on his sleeve and choreographs it into his celebrations, he is in effect hijacking history's verdict. True greatness is usually defined by future generations, not self-issued. Ronaldo's arrogance is the textbook failing of this kind of superstar.

Verdict: A spectacular hat-trick against Spain, yes — but delivered with a celebration that laid Cristiano's runaway ego bare. The 2018 World Cup still ended as a collective failure, goal-laden opener notwithstanding.

⚠️ Disclaimer:The reading of the celebration involves subjectivity; the 'arrogant' verdict is one strand of public opinion, not a final word.
