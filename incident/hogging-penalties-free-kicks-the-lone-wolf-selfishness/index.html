Hogging Penalties & Free-Kicks — The Lone-Wolf Selfishness

Location Multiple clubs / national team
Category Off-pitch
Severity

Penalty monopoly, the receipts: In Real Madrid's 'BBC' era Ronaldo took virtually every penalty going. Gareth Bale put it on record: 'I competed with him on free-kicks, I always won in training, but in matches the penalties and free-kicks all went to him, it's not fair.' One complaint that said everything about life in a superstar's shadow.

Free-kick hegemony: In a single club season Ronaldo racked up 135 shots, to Bale's 50 and Benzema's 60. Free-kicks were his 'private reserve' — no matter how absurd the distance or angle, there he was over the ball in the signature pose, teammates watching.

The cold war with Bale: In the 2014-15 season Bale was caught on camera time and again, visibly unhappy, as Ronaldo lined up the free-kicks — the contest over duties all but white-hot. Media tallies had Bale's free-kick conversion rate far higher than Ronaldo's. The ball still never went to him.

Benzema's forbearance: The 'supporting' forward spent years as Ronaldo's foil and shield. Cameras once caught Benzema scoring, only for Ronaldo to complain to the referee about not getting the assist — team spirit reduced to a punchline.

Stats over team: Ronaldo's obsession with his personal goal tally bordered on the pathological — he once walked off visibly unhappy after a big win in which he had not scored. Real legend Guti said it outright: 'He sometimes rates his personal stats above the team's win.' That self-centred value system poisoned the dressing room.

Continued at Portugal: At international level he monopolised penalties and free-kicks all the same. At the 2022 World Cup he missed a penalty that disrupted Portugal's rhythm; at Euro 2024 he missed another and wept on the pitch, personal pride placed above the team while teammates had to come and console him.

The price of selfishness: When a player puts 'my goals' above 'our wins', so-called leadership becomes a fig leaf for autocracy. Bale eventually chose to leave, Benzema shouldered the blame for years, and the BBC trio's breakup was not unconnected to this monopoly on resources.
